### 4. PRIORITIZATION FRAMEWORKS

**RICE Scoring**
- Reach: How many users affected (per time period)
- Impact: How much it improves their experience (0.25 / 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 3)
- Confidence: How certain are we (% as decimal)
- Effort: Person-months required
- Score = (Reach × Impact × Confidence) / Effort

**Value vs. Effort Matrix**
- High Value, Low Effort: Do immediately
- High Value, High Effort: Plan carefully
- Low Value, Low Effort: Nice-to-haves
- Low Value, High Effort: Avoid

**Kano Model**
- Must-haves: Basic expectations (dissatisfiers if absent)
- Performance: Linear satisfaction (more is better)
- Delighters: Unexpected features (non-linear satisfaction)

**Opportunity Scoring**
- Survey users on importance AND satisfaction
- Prioritize high importance, low satisfaction (biggest gaps)

**ICE Scoring**
- Impact: Business impact (1-10)
- Confidence: Certainty of impact (1-10)
- Ease: Effort required (1-10)
- Score = (Impact × Confidence) / Effort

## DECISION-MAKING FRAMEWORKS

### Build vs. Buy vs. Partner
**Evaluation Criteria:**
- Core competency alignment
- Time to market urgency
- Total cost of ownership
- Customization requirements
- Integration complexity
- Strategic importance
- Market availability

**Decision Matrix:**
- Build: Core differentiator, unique requirements, long-term strategic
- Buy: Commodity capability, proven solutions available, faster
- Partner: Specialized expertise, ecosystem play, risk sharing

### Minimum Viable Product (MVP) Definition

**MVP is NOT:**
- A crappy product
- A beta version
- All features at low quality

**MVP IS:**
- Minimum set of features needed to learn
- Sufficient quality for early adopters
- Focused on one core hypothesis
- Fast to market for validation

**How to Scope MVP:**
1. Identify core value proposition
2. Define minimum feature set to deliver that value
3. Remove everything else (be ruthless)
4. Ensure quality bar for included features
5. Define success metrics for learning

### Feature Flag Strategy

**When to Use Feature Flags:**
- Large features (gradual rollout)
- Risky changes (easy rollback)
- A/B tests (percentage rollout)
- Beta programs (specific user access)
- Kill switches (emergency disable)

**Best Practices:**
- Name flags clearly (feature_name_enabled)
- Document flag purpose and owner
- Set expiration dates
- Clean up old flags regularly
- Monitor flag-based cohorts separately

## COMMUNICATION EXCELLENCE

### Writing Principles

**Clarity Above All**
- Use simple words (prefer "use" over "utilize")
- Short sentences (15-20 words average)
- Active voice ("we will build" not "will be built")
- Specific language ("increase conversion by 15%" not "improve conversion")
- Avoid jargon unless necessary (define when used)

**Structure for Scanning**
- Start with executive summary
- Use headings and subheadings liberally
- Bullet points for lists
- Bold key terms and metrics
- Callout boxes for critical information
- Visual hierarchy (not walls of text)

**Persuasive Writing**
- Lead with WHY (business case)
- Support with WHAT (solution)
- Follow with HOW (implementation)
- Address objections proactively
- Use data to reinforce intuition
- Tell stories with customer quotes

### Meeting Facilitation

**Sprint Planning**
- Prepare prioritized backlog beforehand
- Review sprint goal and capacity
- Team commits to stories (not assigned)
- Timebox to 2 hours per sprint week
- End with clear commitment

**Backlog Refinement**
- Review upcoming stories
- Break down large stories
- Add acceptance criteria
- Identify dependencies
- Rough estimation
- Continuous activity (not just weekly meeting)

**Sprint Review/Demo**
- Show working software (not slides)
- Collect feedback from stakeholders
- Discuss what worked and what didn't
- Adjust backlog based on learnings
- Celebrate team accomplishments

**1-on-1s with Engineers**
- Understand blockers and frustrations
- Get feedback on PRD quality
- Discuss technical approach
- Build trust and rapport
- Career development discussions

## ANTI-PATTERNS TO AVOID

### PRD Anti-Patterns
❌ Solution disguised as requirement ("The button must be blue")
✅ Problem statement ("Users are confused about submit action")

❌ Vague requirements ("Make it fast")
✅ Specific targets ("Page load < 2 seconds, 95th percentile")

❌ Kitchen sink approach (too many features)
✅ Focused scope (MVP mindset)

❌ No success metrics
✅ Clear, measurable outcomes

❌ Writing for yourself
✅ Writing for all stakeholders (eng, design, QA, etc.)

### Process Anti-Patterns
❌ **Feature Factory**: Building without validating impact
✅ **Outcome-Driven**: Measure results, iterate based on data

❌ **HiPPO Decisions**: Highest Paid Person's Opinion
✅ **Evidence-Based**: Data + user research + strategic alignment

❌ **Waterfall PRD**: 50-page document, then over the wall
✅ **Collaborative Discovery**: Iterative, involving team early

❌ **Date-Driven**: Commit to dates before understanding scope
✅ **Scope-Driven**: Define MVP, estimate realistically, adjust dates

❌ **Build Trap**: In love with building, not solving problems
✅ **Problem-Focused**: Validate problem before building solution

### Stakeholder Anti-Patterns
❌ **The Order Taker**: Just executing requests
✅ **The Partner**: Challenging requests with data and alternatives

❌ **The Dictator**: "Just build what I say"
✅ **The Facilitator**: "Let's discover the best solution together"

❌ **The Yes Person**: Saying yes to everything
✅ **The Strategic No**: Saying no with empathy and alternatives

### User Interview Script Template

**Opening (5 min)**
- Thank participant
- Explain purpose and confidentiality
- Set expectations (no right/wrong answers)
- Ask permission to record
- Request thinking out loud

**Background (10 min)**
- Tell me about your role
- Walk me through a typical day
- What are your main goals?
- What challenges do you face?

**Current State (15 min)**
- How do you currently [do the job-to-be-done]?
- Walk me through the last time you [specific task]
- What works well about that process?
- What's frustrating about it?
- Have you tried any alternatives?

**Solution Testing (20 min)** (if showing prototype)
- I'm going to show you [concept]. Think out loud.
- What would you expect this to do?
- Try to [complete task]
- What's confusing?
- What's missing?
- How does this compare to your current approach?

**Closing (10 min)**
- If you had a magic wand, what would you change?
- What haven't I asked that I should have?
- Can I follow up with you after we build this?
- Thank you + incentive

## FRAMEWORKS FOR SPECIFIC SITUATIONS

### Handling Disagreements with Stakeholders

**Step 1: Understand Completely**
- "Help me understand your perspective on [issue]"
- "What concerns you most about [proposal]?"
- Listen actively, don't defend

**Step 2: Find Common Ground**
- "I think we both agree that [shared goal]"
- "We're aligned on [principle]"

**Step 3: Present Data**
- "Here's what the research shows..."
- "The data indicates..."
- "Users told us..."

**Step 4: Propose Test**
- "What if we ran a small experiment?"
- "Could we test both approaches with 50/50 split?"
- "What evidence would change your mind?"

**Step 5: Escalate if Needed**
- "I respect your experience, and we disagree on approach"
- "Let's bring this to [decision maker] with both perspectives"

### Saying No to Feature Requests

**The Empathetic No Framework:**

1. **Acknowledge**: "I understand why this is important to you"
2. **Context**: "Given our current priorities of [X, Y, Z]..."
3. **Trade-off**: "Building this would mean not doing [A, B, C]"
4. **Alternative**: "What if we [alternative approach]?"
5. **Timeline**: "Let's revisit this in [timeframe] based on [conditions]"

**Example:**
"I understand this feature would help close more enterprise deals. Given our current focus on improving retention (which impacts 100% of customers vs 5% for this feature), building this now would delay our onboarding improvements by 6 weeks. What if we document this as a workaround for sales, and revisit after Q2 when we can dedicate more resources? That way we don't sacrifice the retention work that's critical to our unit economics."

### Driving Consensus in Meetings

**The Decision-Making Framework:**

1. **State the Decision**: "By end of this meeting, we need to decide [X]"
2. **Share Context**: Present data, research, options analyzed
3. **Hear Perspectives**: "What's your take?" to each stakeholder
4. **Synthesize**: "I'm hearing [themes]"
5. **Propose Decision**: "Based on [reasoning], I recommend [decision]"
6. **Confirm**: "Any blocking concerns?" (not seeking unanimous agreement)
7. **Document**: "Here's what we decided and why"
8. **Commit**: Even if you disagree, commit to the decision

## MENTAL MODELS & PRINCIPLES

### First Principles Thinking
Don't accept "that's how we've always done it"
- Break down problem to fundamental truths
- Reason up from there
- Challenge assumptions

### Jobs-to-be-Done (JTBD)
People don't want products, they want progress
- Focus on the job, not the product
- Understand functional, emotional, and social jobs
- "When I [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome]"

### Pareto Principle (80/20 Rule)
- 80% of value from 20% of features
- Focus on the vital few, not trivial many
- Use data to identify the 20%

### Opportunity Cost
Every yes is a no to something else
- Make trade-offs explicit
- Quantify what you're giving up
- Ensure trade-offs are worth it

### Sunk Cost Fallacy
Don't throw good money after bad
- Past investment doesn't justify future investment
- Kill features that aren't working
- Pivot based on new information

### Network Effects
Some products get better with more users
- Direct: Value increases as more users join
- Indirect: More users attract complementary services
- Data: More usage improves product (AI/ML)
